PROJECT OVERVIEW · HEALTHCARE ANALYTICS

ED-to-ICU
Escalation Modeling.

An applied data science capstone exploring emergency department patient trajectories and the transition to intensive care.

ACADEMIC PROJECTMIMIC-IV · 2008–2019

01 — THE QUESTION

Understanding
escalation.

How can patient information available during an emergency department visit help characterize the risk and timing of subsequent ICU escalation?

Project context

The emergency department is a dynamic environment where clinicians make decisions with incomplete and evolving information. This project examines ED-to-ICU transitions as both a prediction problem and a sequential decision-making problem.

Data source

The analysis uses the MIMIC-IV dataset, a de-identified electronic health record resource. ED and hospital data were prepared to represent patient visits and relevant events over time, with attention to temporal integrity and the information available at each decision point.

02 — APPROACH

From snapshots
to sequences.

01

Data preparation

Construct ED visit cohorts, assess data quality, and prepare time-aware clinical features.

02

Predictive modeling

Develop supervised models to examine ED disposition and escalation-related outcomes.

03

Sequential modeling

Represent patient stays as event-driven sequences to study how risk information changes over time.

04

Policy evaluation

Explore offline reinforcement learning policies and evaluate their behavior in the context of escalation decisions.

03 — TOOLS & METHODS

Technical focus.

  • Python
  • SQL
  • MIMIC-IV
  • Data Engineering
  • EDA
  • Scikit-learn
  • Classification
  • LSTM
  • Offline RL
  • Model Evaluation

Scope and limitations

This is an academic research project using retrospective, de-identified data. It is not a clinically validated tool, does not provide medical advice, and should not be used to guide patient care. Model outputs and policy evaluations are exploratory and require further validation before any real-world application.
